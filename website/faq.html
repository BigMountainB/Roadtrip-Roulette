FAQ

What is Road Trip Roulette?
A roguelite driving game about a broke musician racing 293 real Washington miles — Seattle to Pullman — to make the gig of a lifetime. Ten music genres, each with its own car, handling, economy quirks, and original soundtrack. Manage fuel, food, sleep, the engine, and the law; die (or arrive) and spin it again.
What platforms is it on?
PC via Steam is the primary target, with a mobile version planned after launch. The free browser demo on this site runs anywhere a modern browser does.
When does it come out? How much will it cost?
Release date and price haven't been announced yet. The demo is free, forever.
What's in the demo?
The first leg of the route — Seattle to North Bend, about 32 miles — with two genres to choose from. Full-game saves, the other eight genres, and the rest of the state wait for the full release.
Is the route real?
Yes — the game follows the actual drive: I-90 from Seattle over Snoqualmie Pass, down to the Columbia at Vantage, WA-26 across the scablands, and US-195 into Pullman. All 19 rest-stop towns are real places with real facts on their welcome signs. (The crime is fictional. Mostly.)
What's my username? How does the leaderboard work?
Your license plate is your public name. Plates are unique worldwide — first driver to claim one keeps it. The game submits your best score, longest run, and fastest Seattle→Pullman completion to the world leaderboard automatically.
Is the soundtrack original?
Yes — 100+ original tracks written for the game across all ten genres, from phonk to norteño. You can listen to every one of them on the Genres page.
Is there multiplayer?
No — it's a single-player game. The competition is the leaderboard, and the passenger seat is for the amp.
